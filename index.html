How to Use This App
5/3/1 by Jim Wendler — a simple, proven strength program

Getting Started

  1. 1 Enter your 1-rep max (1RM) for each lift in the input fields at the top and click Generate Charts. This is the most weight you can lift once with good form.
  2. 2 Don't know your 1RM? No problem — use the 1RM Calculator at the bottom of the page. Enter a weight you can lift for a few reps and it'll estimate your max for you.
  3. 3 The app sets your Training Max to 90% of your 1RM. All your working weights are calculated from this number — it keeps progress conservative and sustainable.
  4. 4 Select a week tab to see that week's sets and weights. After finishing all 4 (or 5) weeks, bump each lift by 5 lbs (upper body) or 10 bs (lower body) and repeat.

The Weekly Cycle

Week Scheme Notes
Week 1 5 / 5 / 5+ Three working sets. The "+" on the last set means do as many reps as you can.
Week 2 3 / 3 / 3+ Heavier weights, fewer reps. Same idea — push the last set.
Week 3 5 / 3 / 1+ Your heaviest week. Go all-out on the final set.
Week 4 Deload Light weight, 10 reps each set. Let your body recover — don't skip it.
Week 5 Hypertrophy Moderate weights, 10 reps per set. Great for building muscle size.

Templates (5/3/1 Flavors)

Pick a template to add supplemental work after the three main sets. Supplemental work applies to weeks 1-3 only; the deload and hypertrophy weeks are unaffected.

Template Supplemental Notes
Normal 5/3/1 None Just the three main work sets. Add your own assistance.
BBB 5 × 10 @ 50% Boring But Big - high-volume hypertrophy on the main lift.
FSL 5 × 5 @ first-set % First Set Last - reuses the week's opening work-set weight.
SSL 5 × 5 @ second-set % Second Set Last - a heavier supplemental than FSL.
Triumvirate 2 assistance lifts Main lift plus two listed accessory exercises per lift.

Progress Tracker

Log your actual top-set performance each week and watch your estimated 1RM trend over time.

  • Log Set on each lift card records the reps you actually hit on that week's final work set, then estimates your 1RM from the weight and reps you entered.
  • Logging the same lift and week on the same day overwrites that entry, so re-logging a set you mis-typed won't create a duplicate point. Logging a different week on the same calendar day is kept as its own point.
  • The chart below the lift cards plots each lift's estimated 1RM over time — use the colored tabs to switch which lift is shown, and hover a point for its exact date, weight, and reps.
  • The table under the chart lists every logged entry. Click into the date, weight, or reps cells to edit an entry directly — editing weight or reps recalculates the 1RM automatically. Each row also has a delete button to remove an entry.
  • Progress data is stored separately from your 1RM/Training Max data, so clearing or regenerating your charts won't erase your logged history. Use the Export/Import controls on the Progress Tracker card to back it up or move it to another device.

Tips & Features

  • Click any lift card to open a full expanded view with plate-by-plate breakdowns for every set.
  • Hover over a weight to see a tooltip showing exactly which plates to load on each side of the bar.
  • Click a working set row (in the expanded view) to highlight it as completed — handy for tracking during a session.
  • Warm-up sets appear in muted text above the working sets. Do them — they prime your joints and nervous system.
  • Export / Import lets you save your 1RM data as a JSON file for backup or to move between devices.
  • Theme picker at the bottom lets you switch between a wide range of color themes.
  • The Reps-Needed Calculator at the bottom figures out how many reps at a given weight you'd need to hit a target 1RM.
